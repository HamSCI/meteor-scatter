# meteor-scatter in F32: one I/Q channel, decode from it, keep the I/Q of every decoded slot

**Authors:** Michael Hauan (AC0G) + Claude
**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Status:** design, approved by Michael 2026-11-06 ("Yes. Let's go with that.").  Michael decided with Rob
Robinett to move meteor-scatter to F32.

## Objective (Michael, 2026-11-06)

"Among the objectives of this effort we provide everything the traditional MSQP does with the addition of
better metadata, more efficient CPU usage, and higher quality archive data for post-analysis."

## Why

Today each meteor-scatter channel asks radiod for 12 kHz USB audio, and it already asks for F32 on the
wire, whatever the config's `encoding` says (receiver_manager.py forces it).  It then peak-normalizes
each 15 s slot and writes it as a 16-bit mono WAV, runs `jt9 --msk144` on it, and keeps the WAV only
when jt9 decodes something.  Two things get lost along the way:

- **Rounding.**  The 16-bit WAV rounds every sample before jt9 sees it.  A weak ping near the bottom of
  the range loses some of itself to that rounding.
- **The trail.**  Audio keeps amplitude only.  The phase and Doppler of the reflection from a meteor
  trail, which after-action analysis wants, never reach the disk.

F32 I/Q keeps both.  We want to show that decodes from F32 match today's or better, and to keep
recordings worth analysing afterwards.

## What changes

1. **One channel per frequency, in F32 I/Q.**  meteor-scatter asks radiod for one complex F32 channel per
   MSK144 frequency (the `iq` preset), not one USB audio channel (the `usb` preset).  radiod does no more work than now, which matters on
   stations already short of CPU (KFS-OMNI ran its decoder cores at 99 % on 2026-11-06).
2. **meteor-scatter makes the audio.**  From each 15 s slot of I/Q it makes the real USB audio that jt9
   expects, at 12 kHz, in F32.
   - The channel's filter keeps only the upper sideband, about +50 Hz to +3 kHz above the dial frequency,
     as the `usb` preset does.  The real part of that one-sided complex signal then *is* the USB audio.
   - The details are left to the builder, under one rule: the audio must decode as well as radiod's own
     USB audio does (see Proof).
3. **jt9 decodes F32.**  UberSDR's jt9 accepts a 32-bit float WAV for MSK144, as it already does for FT8,
   FT4 and FT2.  This is a deliberate step away from the WSJT-X reference, which refuses float input.
   - The 16-bit path keeps byte-for-byte parity, and its 395 comparison cases stay as they are.
   - The float path joins the ubersdr_wsprd `msk144` branch, documented as ours.
   - It goes upstream only if madpsy wants it.
4. **Keep only what decodes.**  A slot with no decode is discarded, as now.  A slot with a decode keeps its
   I/Q, not audio, beside the same JSON sidecar as today.
5. **One kept slot, one file.**  (Claude's call.)  A two-channel 32-bit float WAV, I then Q, at the
   channel's sample rate.  Any audio or DSP tool opens it.  Digital RF stays the format for uploads to
   wsprdaemon.org (the event-response design, sigmond `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-06-event-response-design.md`).

## What every kept slot's sidecar carries (Michael, 2026-11-06)

"Sidecar should carry definitive timing as well as front-end gain adjustments so analysis can adjust for
those, if necessary."

- **Timing.**  The slot's exact start in UTC (not rounded), the RTP timestamp of its first sample, and
  the sample rate.  Then the anchor behind those labels: its source, whether it came from radiod's RTP
  clock or fell back to the host clock, and the offset applied.  And the client contract's
  `timing_authority_applied` block, as it stood for this slot's labels: tier, sigma, offset, snapshot
  age.  Built from the anchor that labelled the slot, never from a fresh read.
- **Front-end gain.**  Every stage between the antenna and the file, enough to put the stored samples back
  on an absolute scale:
  - the RX888's RF gain, attenuation and AGC state, and any change during the slot
  - the channel's gain and AGC as requested and as radiod reports them
  - the calibration (`scale_db`) and any gain meteor-scatter applied to the decoder's audio
  - the stored I/Q itself carries no gain of its own

## Sizes

| Kept slot (15 s) | Size |
|---|---|
| Today: 12 kHz mono 16-bit WAV | 0.36 MB |
| F32 I/Q at 12 kHz complex | 1.44 MB |
| Half-precision I/Q (float16), if tested and adopted later | 0.72 MB |

Kept only on decode, our busiest 6 m receiver (168 decoded slots a day, 10-13 to 11-06) would store about
240 MB a day.

## Proof before any station switches

Run the old and new paths side by side on the same station through a morning's traffic, 09:00–12:00Z, on
UACNJ or another busy 6 m receiver.  One way: two meteor-scatter instances on the same radiod, one on the
old `usb`/`s16be` channel and one on the new F32 I/Q channel.

- The new path must decode at least the slots the old one decodes, with SNR the same or better.
- Every slot that only one path decodes gets listed and examined.
- The CPU of both paths is measured.

## Not in this change

- **Packaging for the Meteor Scatter QSO Party** (Michael, 2026-11-06: "We need ALL.TXT, but the I/Q we
  store permits recovery of a 16-bit int mono WAV.  We don't need to provide it in the latter form as it
  is derivable from the former, if needed.").  `msqp package` delivers ALL.TXT, the kept I/Q files and
  their sidecars.  No 16-bit WAVs.  `meteor-scatter msqp wav <slot>` derives the 12 kHz 16-bit mono
  WAV from a kept I/Q file on demand (USB audio as radiod makes it, peak-normalised as today).  The I/Q
  is four times the size of the WAVs it replaces, so `msqp totals` measures against Zenodo's 50 GB
  record limit.
- **Float16 storage.**  It is a later, separate test.  It needs a check that the tools in the chain accept
  it (Digital RF, numpy, the MSQP tooling).
- **Running jt9 resident (`--feed`) instead of once per slot.**  It is worth measuring, and it is separate.

## Left to the builder

- How meteor-scatter makes the USB audio from the I/Q: radiod's filter edges, any further filtering, and
  scaling to the level jt9 expects.  Decode quality judges it.
- The F32 WAV reader in jt9 for MSK144: scale and conversion, matching how the port already reads 16-bit.
- Config keys, the per-instance migration, and the sidecar fields that record the I/Q's sample rate,
  centre frequency and encoding.
